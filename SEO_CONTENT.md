## What is Nickel Release Calculator, EN 1811?

The Nickel Release Calculator, EN 1811 is a free web tool developed by Poli International that estimates nickel release rates from body jewelry alloys and checks compliance against the EU EN 1811 piercing limit of 0.2 µg/cm²/week (REACH Annex XVII, Entry 27).

The tool provides two main functions:

1. **Material Lookup**: Search a database of 15 common jewelry materials to find their estimated nickel content, release rate, and EN 1811 compliance status. Materials include implant-grade alloys, gold, silver, polymers, and non-compliant metals.

2. **Custom Calculator**: Enter a known nickel content percentage (0-100%) and select a surface finish to estimate the release rate using an empirical model calibrated against EN 1811 literature data. Optionally enter surface area to calculate total weekly nickel load.

The calculator displays results with visual gauge bars showing where the estimated release falls relative to both the piercing limit (0.2 µg/cm²/week) and the skin contact limit (0.5 µg/cm²/week). Each result includes actionable compliance recommendations.

## Who Should Use This Tool

**Tattoo and piercing studio owners**: Verify that the jewelry you stock and sell meets EU nickel release requirements. Use the material lookup to quickly check common alloys before purchasing bulk inventory.

**Body jewelry manufacturers and distributors**: Screen alloy compositions during product development. The custom calculator helps estimate release rates from known nickel content before commissioning expensive EN 1811 certified testing.

**Piercing artists**: Educate clients about material safety. Show clients why certain materials are recommended for initial piercings versus healed piercings. Demonstrate the difference between compliant and non-compliant alloys.

**Clients with nickel allergies**: Self-check jewelry materials before purchase. Understand which alloys are safe for sensitive skin. Learn why "surgical steel" without certification may be risky.

**Quality assurance and compliance officers**: Perform preliminary compliance checks on jewelry lots. Document estimated release rates for internal quality records before sending samples for certified testing.

## How to Use

### Material Lookup Tab

1. **Enter a material name** in the search field. Examples: "titanium", "white gold", "surgical steel", "nickel silver", "BioFlex"
2. **Click "Look Up"** or press Enter
3. **Read the material card** that appears:
   - Check the compliance badge (Compliant, Borderline, Non-Compliant, Unverified)
   - View the estimated release rate in µg/cm²/week
   - See the nickel content percentage
   - Read the release note explaining the result
   - Click the card header to expand and view additional details (standards, manufacturer, alternative names)
4. **Use the accordion** to expand/collapse material details by clicking the card header

### Custom Calculator Tab

1. **Enter nickel content** (0-100%) in the first field. Example: 13.5 for 316LVM stainless steel
2. **Select surface finish** from the dropdown:
   - Mirror polished (lowest release)
   - Standard / machine finished (default)
   - Brushed / satin
   - Rough / cast
   - Scratched / worn (highest release)
3. **Optionally enter surface area** in cm² to calculate total weekly nickel load
4. **Click "Calculate"**
5. **Interpret the results**:
   - Read the estimated release rate in µg/cm²/week
   - View the gauge bars showing position relative to both limits
   - Check the compliance badges for piercing and skin contact limits
   - Read the recommendation for next steps

## Real-World Use Cases

### Use Case 1: Verifying Surgical Steel for Initial Piercings

**Scenario**: A piercing studio owner wants to confirm that their 316LVM implant-grade steel jewelry is safe for initial piercings.

**Inputs**:
- Tab: Material Lookup
- Search: "implant steel" or "ASTM F138"

**Result**:
- Material: Implant-Grade Steel (ASTM F138)
- Nickel content: 13.5%
- Estimated release: 0.08 µg/cm²/week
- Compliance: Compliant (Pass)
- Note: Despite 10-14% nickel content, the passive oxide layer restricts release below 0.2 µg/cm²/week

**Action**: The owner can confidently use this material for initial piercings but should still obtain a certified EN 1811 test report for regulatory compliance documentation.

### Use Case 2: Comparing White Gold Alloys for Healed Piercings

**Scenario**: A client wants a white gold nose stud for a healed piercing. The piercer needs to check if nickel-based white gold is safe.

**Inputs**:
- Tab: Material Lookup
- Search: "white gold 18k"

**Result**:
- Material: White Gold 18k
- Nickel content: 12%
- Estimated release: 0.35 µg/cm²/week
- Compliance: Borderline
- Note: Release is close to or at the 0.2 µg/cm²/week limit. Surface condition could push release above threshold.

**Action**: The piercer recommends palladium-based white gold (0% nickel) instead. If the client insists on nickel-based white gold, the piercer advises against use in fresh piercings and recommends monitoring for reaction in healed piercings.

### Use Case 3: Checking Nickel Silver Costume Jewelry

**Scenario**: A client brings in a nickel silver belly ring from a street vendor and asks if it is safe.

**Inputs**:
- Tab: Material Lookup
- Search: "nickel silver"

**Result**:
- Material: Nickel Silver / German Silver
- Nickel content: 18%
- Estimated release: 4.5 µg/cm²/week
- Compliance: Non-Compliant (Fail)
- Note: Release is up to 22× the 0.2 limit. Completely non-compliant with EN 1811.

**Action**: The piercer explains that nickel silver contains no actual silver and is dangerous for body jewelry. They recommend switching to implant-grade titanium or BioFlex polymer and discard the nickel silver piece.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Q1: What is the EN 1811 standard?
EN 1811:2011+A1:2015 is the European standard "Reference test method for release of nickel from all post assemblies which are inserted into pierced parts of the human body." It defines the test method for measuring nickel release from jewelry.

### Q2: What is the difference between nickel content and nickel release?
Nickel content is the percentage of nickel in the alloy by mass. Nickel release is the amount of nickel that actually leaches from the surface when exposed to artificial sweat under EN 1811 test conditions. A material can have high nickel content but low release due to a protective oxide layer (e.g., 316LVM stainless steel).

### Q3: Can I use this calculator instead of a certified EN 1811 test?
No. This calculator provides estimates for guidance only. To demonstrate EN 1811 compliance for product sale in the EU, you must obtain a certified test report from an accredited laboratory. The calculator helps you screen materials before commissioning expensive testing.

### Q4: What does the 0.2 µg/cm²/week limit mean?
This is the maximum allowable nickel release for items inserted into pierced parts of the human body, as defined by REACH Annex XVII, Entry 27 (EU Directive 94/27/EC, amended by 2004/96/EC). It applies to both initial piercings and healed piercings.

### Q5: Why does surface finish affect nickel release?
Rougher surfaces have more exposed surface area at the microscopic level and more defects in the protective oxide layer. Scratched or worn surfaces can release 3-5 times more nickel than mirror-polished surfaces of the same alloy.

### Q6: Is "surgical steel" always safe for piercings?
No. "Surgical steel" is a marketing term, not a certified grade. Without a material certificate and EN 1811 test report, nickel release cannot be quantified. Some unspecified surgical steels release over 1 µg/cm²/week. Always use certified implant-grade material (ASTM F138) or BioFlex polymer.

### Q7: What materials have zero nickel release?
BioFlex polymer, PTFE (Teflon), nylon/acrylic, implant-grade titanium (ASTM F136), niobium, platinum, and some gold alloys (yellow 18k/14k, palladium-based white gold) have zero or negligible nickel release.

### Q8: Can I calculate total weekly nickel exposure?
Yes. In the Custom Calculator tab, enter the surface area of the jewelry piece in cm². The tool will calculate total weekly nickel load in µg/week. For example, a 2.5 cm² piece with 0.08 µg/cm²/week release gives 0.2 µg total weekly exposure.

### Q9: What should I do if my material shows "Borderline" compliance?
Surface condition, scratches, and acidic skin pH could push release above the 0.2 µg/cm²/week threshold. Consider switching to nickel-free alternatives like implant-grade titanium or BioFlex polymer for guaranteed compliance. Commission a certified EN 1811 test before selling.

### Q10: Does the calculator work for all jewelry types?
The calculator is calibrated for body jewelry alloys. The material database covers common piercing jewelry materials. For other jewelry types (earrings, necklaces, bracelets), the skin contact limit of 0.5 µg/cm²/week applies instead of the 0.2 piercing limit.
